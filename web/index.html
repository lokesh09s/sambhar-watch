How radar sees water

NISAR sends microwave pulses down at an angle and records how much comes back. Calm water acts like a mirror: it bounces the pulse away from the satellite, so water looks dark. Land, plants and buildings scatter some of the pulse back, so they look brighter.

Radar works through cloud and at night, which matters at Sambhar because the lake changes most during the monsoon, when optical satellites are often blind.

Darkness alone is not enough: smooth dry salt crust can look as dark as water. So we also use coherence: how much the ground stayed the same between two passes 12 days apart. Water never stays the same, so its coherence is low. Dry crust, soil and buildings stay the same, so theirs is high.

Our provisional rule: a pixel is a dark unstable surface when it is dark in HH and its coherence is low. We call this “water or wet brine”, not simply water, until Sentinel-2 images confirm it. Each date is compared with the pass 12 days later; dates without one are left out.

The detection frequency of a spot is the number of dates it was detected as a dark unstable surface divided by the number of dates with a valid radar observation there.

Limitations

  • The NISAR data are provisional: calibrated but only partly validated by the mission.
  • Wet salt crust and brine are dark and unstable too, so they are counted with water. Dry crust that is dark but stable is removed by the coherence check. Both are tested separately against Sentinel-2.
  • Coherence needs a second pass 12 days later, so dates without one are left out.
  • The areas depend on the chosen thresholds. The shaded band on the chart shows by how much; differences between dates smaller than the band are not reliable.
  • Two radar modes alternate (dual-pol DHDH and quad-pol QPDH). Until we have checked that they agree, they are shown separately.
  • Radar dates are 12 days apart at best, with some longer gaps. The maps show water on those dates, not continuous presence.
  • Radar tells us where the surface is wet, not how deep the water is.
  • Salt-pan boundaries were drawn by hand. “Outside mapped pans” may still include unmapped pans, channels or roads.
  • These are surface observations only. They say nothing about whether any salt pan is legal.
